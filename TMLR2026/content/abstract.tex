Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning can fail when local incentives outweigh the team objective. We introduce a protocol for measuring where this failure occurs as the reward mixture changes. The protocol combines behavioral collapse labels, observed failure proportions, and seed-block uncertainty estimates to locate the \AbstractCrossingProbability{} collapse crossing, $w_{50}$. On an inventory-network testbed with shared-parameter PPO, the crossing is \AbstractCrossing{} at \PrimaryBudget{} agent transitions. Separating reward curvature from a loss-branch multiplier reveals opposing shifts: curvature lowers the crossing, while increasing the multiplier changes it by \LossMultiplierShift{} relative to the curvature-only baseline. An independent longitudinal cohort retains a positive multiplier-minus-curvature difference through \MaxConfirmationBudget{} transitions, while recovery analysis shows that collapse is not absorbing. A direct symmetric-scaling control moves the crossing at least as far as the loss-only multiplier, and the asymmetric-minus-symmetric interval includes zero. The experiment therefore identifies sensitivity to the transformed training signal, not a gain--loss-asymmetry mechanism. These results establish a comparative measurement tool for reward design; numerical crossings remain conditional on the task, learner, training budget, reward units, optimizer, and behavioral label.
