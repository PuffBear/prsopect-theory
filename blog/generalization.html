Is the phase diagram substrate-independent?

Everything so far lives in one environment (a supply chain) under one algorithm (PPO). The obvious objection: maybe the boundary, and loss aversion's grip on it, are artifacts of that substrate. So we changed both — the algorithm and the environment — and asked whether the direction of the effect survives.

Two replications. First, swap the algorithm: re-run the same supply chain under MAPPO (centralized-critic actor–critic) instead of independent PPO. Second, swap the environment entirely: MPE Cooperative Navigation — a spatial multi-agent particle task where agents must spread to cover landmarks, nothing like a supply chain — under MAPPO. In every case we apply the identical prospect-theoretic transform, the identical $w$-mixing, and the identical pre-registered estimator stack (Firth-corrected logistic, bootstrap $n=2000$).

FIG. 8 — The generalization crux. $w_{\mathrm{crit}}(\lambda)$ for three settings on one axis: supply chain under PPO (blue), supply chain under MAPPO (green), and MPE Cooperative Navigation under MAPPO (red). Bars are bootstrap 95% CIs. The absolute level differs — MAPPO and the spatial task sit higher, and MPE's boundary crowds against $w \approx 1$ with wide intervals — but the upward direction with $\lambda$ is shared across both algorithm and environment. Loss aversion raises the coordination boundary regardless of substrate.

Three things to read off it, in decreasing order of confidence.

The direction generalizes. All three series trend upward in $\lambda$: more loss aversion, more team-pay required to coordinate. This is the robust, cross-cutting claim — it holds when we change the credit-assignment machinery (PPO → MAPPO) and when we change the world (supply chain → spatial navigation). The qualitative phase-diagram story is not a supply-chain artifact.

The constants do not. MAPPO's centralized critic shifts the whole boundary up — at $\lambda = 1$ it needs $w_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx 0.80$ where PPO needs $0.59$ — which is exactly what Proposition 1's $(N, \beta, c)$ dependence predicts when you change how the team term is estimated. The form is shared; $(w_\infty, K)$ are substrate-specific. That is the right kind of universality: structure invariant, scale not.

MPE is a directional confirmation, not a second precise phase diagram — and the writeup says so. Cooperative Navigation's collapse boundary sits near $w \approx 1$ with wide, sometimes-unbounded bootstrap intervals (the high-$\lambda$ CIs run past 1.0), and the behavioral decomposition there is unstable across re-estimation — the null $w_{\mathrm{crit}}$ floats between $\approx 0.71$ and $\approx 1.0$ depending on the collapse-threshold calibration. So we report MPE as evidence for the sign of the effect across environments, and explicitly not as a clean $w_{\mathrm{crit}}(\lambda)$ measurement on par with the supply chain. Under independent PPO on MPE the boundary degenerates entirely (the logistic doesn't identify), which is itself informative: the centralized critic is doing real coordination work in the spatial task.

Change the algorithm, change the world — the boundary still climbs with loss aversion. The slope is the finding; the intercept is the substrate.— the cross-environment result, one line
§ 08What this means for AI systems

Your multi-agent system has a phase diagram. You should know where on it you live.

Risk-shaping is not free. The community increasingly trains agents with risk-sensitive objectives — CVaR policy gradients, asymmetric reward clipping, conservative offline penalties, loss-averse reward models distilled from human feedback (humans have $\lambda \approx 2.25$ baked in). Every one of these moves an agent along the $\lambda$ axis. This work says that axis is not a tuning knob with smooth consequences: it has a cliff on it, and the cliff's position rises and saturates with $\lambda$. A safety intervention that raises effective $\lambda$ from 1 to 3 silently consumes $\approx 0.19$ of your coordination margin ($0.586 \to 0.774$); to 7, $\approx 0.27$. If your reward mixing sits at $w = 0.8$ — comfortably coordinated for risk-neutral agents — a high-$\lambda$ population is at or inside the collapse region.

The fragile band is where the danger is. Between the basins, outcomes are metastable: identical systems, identical seeds, different fates under procedure-level perturbations. Production multi-agent systems that "sometimes just fail to cooperate after retraining" may not be flaky — they may be parked on a separatrix. The phase surface gives the diagnosis and the prescription: measure your distance to the boundary, not your average performance.

Decentralization has a price denominated in λ. Federated fleets, autonomous trading desks, multi-robot warehouses — the engineering pressure is always toward local reward (privacy, latency, credit assignment). The boundary prices that pressure: every unit of behavioral risk-asymmetry in your agents raises the minimum centralization that keeps cooperation alive, with diminishing — but, within the measured range, never-vanishing — increments. Whether there is a hard design floor — a $w_\infty < 1$ above which no loss aversion can kill coordination — is the open finalist question from Part 3: if Michaelis–Menten holds, keep $w$ above $\approx 0.9$ and you are safe at any $\lambda$; if odds–power holds, no such floor exists. The next sweep decides, and the answer is a deployable design rule either way.

Interpretability got a foothold. The $\theta < 1$ anomaly is a behavioral fingerprint of policy-level hedging — agents restructuring their exposure rather than their effort. That is precisely the kind of internal mechanism claim that can be audited: compare realized loss-mass distributions across $\lambda$; probe critics for asymmetric value estimates; check whether hedging shows up as inventory buffering in the learned policies. The phase diagram tells us where to look; the theory tells us what we should find.

§ 09Not a future-work section · a research agenda

The next five papers, laid out like a program.

A result invites a follow-up; a law demands a research program. Each paper below has a hypothesis the present work makes precise, a killer experiment, and a reason it can fail informatively. Two of the five are already partway done.

  1. The Law of the Boundary: wcrit(λ) to the extremes.

    Hypothesis: the saturating law holds with $w_\infty < 1$ — loss aversion cannot defeat sufficiently centralized pay. Status: the seven-point sweep is done and confirmed the saturating shape, but left Michaelis–Menten and odds–power statistically tied. Killer experiment: the $\lambda \in \{10, 15, 20\}$ extremes, where the finalists separate by $\approx 0.04$ — several CI-widths. Failure is informative: if odds–power wins, incentive design has no safe floor — arguably the more important headline.

    STATUS: 7-POINT SWEEP COMPLETE · EXTREMES ARE A 3-POINT EXPERIMENT

  2. The Hedging Mechanism: interpreting θ < 1.

    Hypothesis: the sub-linear exponent is caused by endogenous loss-exposure reduction — agents hedge in policy space. Killer experiment: trajectory forensics across λ (realized loss-mass curves, inventory buffering statistics), critic probing for asymmetric value heads, and a causal clamp: freeze exposure (force order distributions) and test whether θ returns to 1. Deliverable: a mechanistic-interpretability account of a behavioral-economics parameter inside a trained policy.

    DATA: ALREADY STORED IN RUN ARCHIVES · THE CLAMP NEEDS ONE NEW SWEEP

  3. Universality: is the phase diagram substrate-independent?

    Hypothesis: the qualitative diagram (sharp boundary, lazy basin, λ-shift) is universal across cooperative substrates; the constants $(w_\infty, K)$ are substrate-specific but the form is not. Status: first evidence in hand — the direction generalizes across PPO→MAPPO and supply-chain→MPE (§ 07, Fig. 8). Killer experiment: add Melting Pot harvest and SMAC-lite, then collapse all curves onto one master curve under the rescaling $\tilde{w} = w/w_\infty$, $\tilde{\lambda} = \lambda/K$. If transition widths share a scaling exponent, this becomes a critical-phenomena claim in earnest.

    STATUS: 2 OF ≥4 SUBSTRATES DONE · MAIN REMAINING COST: COMPUTE

  4. Crossing back: interventions that move the boundary on purpose.

    Hypothesis: the boundary is controllable — annealed centralization schedules ($w$ high early, decayed after basin capture) and asymmetric advantage clipping can place a system on the coordinated side at deployment-time $w$ far below $w_{\mathrm{crit}}$. Killer experiment: a 2×2 of {anneal, clip} × {λ=3, 7}, scored by post-anneal stability under perturbation; Proposition 3(iii) predicts the required anneal duration scales like $\rho^{-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)$ — a quantitative, falsifiable schedule.

    THEORY ALREADY PREDICTS THE SCHEDULE SHAPE — RARE AND WORTH ADVERTISING

  5. Mixed populations: one loss-averse agent in a risk-neutral team.

    Hypothesis: collapse is nucleated — a single high-λ agent shifts the team's effective boundary disproportionately, because the lazy basin grows fastest near the most loss-averse member (heterogeneous $\hat{x}_i$ in the Prop. 2 framework). Killer experiment: hold team mean λ fixed, vary its variance; measure $w_{\mathrm{crit}}$(distribution). Direct implications for human–AI teams, where the human carries λ ≈ 2.25 whether you like it or not — and for adversarial robustness: can one poisoned agent collapse a fleet?

    THE HUMAN–AI FRAMING IS THE BROADEST-AUDIENCE PAPER OF THE FIVE
§ 10Methods, in one honest breath

Pre-registered experiments, 2,500+ controlled runs across PPO and MAPPO on a six-node multi-echelon supply chain (node 1 fixed demand, nodes 2–6 learning) and MPE Cooperative Navigation. Binary collapse criterion frozen before the main sweeps. Critical points from logistic regression of collapse on $w$ (Firth-corrected where quasi-separated); uncertainty from run-level bootstrap, $n = 2{,}000$, clustered by seed; Wilson intervals on cell fractions. The λ-sweep uses $\alpha = \beta = 0.88$, so its $\lambda = 1$ baseline ($w_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0.586$) differs from the null model's $0.685$ — that difference is the curvature effect, isolated by design, not a discrepancy. The seven-point PPO sweep combines the original Exp B cells ($\lambda \in \{1,3,7\}$) with the dense in-between points ($\lambda \in \{2,4,5,6\}$), 100 runs per λ. At high λ the logistic is quasi-separated; we report the bootstrap critical point and suppress the slope. The 600k-step horizon estimate is reported only as a bound ($w_{\mathrm{crit}} < 0.50$) because the w-grid bracketed the transition poorly. The MPE boundary sits near $w \approx 1$ with wide, threshold-sensitive intervals and is reported as a directional cross-environment check, not a precise second phase diagram. All raw run CSVs, pre-registrations, and analysis scripts are in the project archive; every number in this post traces to a file.8

3. The raw 600k logistic returns an unphysical point estimate (−0.60) because the grid lies almost entirely above the transition; the honest statement is the bound. Narrow grids are how you accidentally manufacture precision.

4. The curvature effect's CI, [−0.172, −0.033], excludes zero from above — significant and beneficial. Diminishing sensitivity compresses the perceived cost asymmetry of cooperating. Behavioral bias is not uniformly an enemy of coordination; that intuition dies here.
