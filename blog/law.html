Assume the result is true. What law generates it?

A result says "the boundary moved." A law says "here is the function, here are its parameters, here is the number it forces at $\lambda = 20$ — go falsify it." We enumerated twenty candidate scaling laws $w_{\mathrm{crit}}(\lambda)$ before the data, then fit every one against the measured critical points, weighted by their bootstrap intervals.6

The first version of this note had three points and a queued sweep. The sweep has since returned. We now have seven measured critical points spanning $\lambda \in \{1,\dots,7\}$ — and they change the verdict in a way three points could not have.

WHAT THE SEVEN-POINT SWEEP SETTLED — AND WHAT IT DIDN'T The boundary rises monotonically and saturates: $w_{\mathrm{crit}}$ climbs from $0.586$ at $\lambda=1$ to $0.851$ at $\lambda=7$, with the increments shrinking — exactly the concave, ceiling-bound shape the saturating laws predict. Every two-parameter law now passes the pre-committed CI test. But the decisive separation we hoped for did not arrive: the leader (reciprocal-approach) beats the runner-up by only $\Delta\mathrm{AICc} \approx 0.2$, far short of the pre-registered $\ge 4$. The shape is a law; the exact functional form is still underdetermined within this λ-range. The honest headline is the saturation, not a single winning equation.

Read the scoreboard carefully, because what it does not say is the point.

One — the shape is settled. The two-parameter saturating forms — Michaelis–Menten, reciprocal-approach — sit within every bootstrap interval, with a $w_\infty$ in the $0.89$–$0.93$ range. Whatever the precise equation, the boundary bends toward a ceiling below 1. Seven points are enough to assert that; three were not.

Two — the finalists still disagree about the only thing that matters for AI systems, and the data cannot yet referee them. Michaelis–Menten (and reciprocal) say the boundary saturates below 1: even infinitely loss-averse agents coordinate if pay is $\ge w_\infty \approx 0.9$. Odds–power says no ceiling — $w_{\mathrm{crit}} \to 1$, so sufficient loss aversion defeats any incentive design. At $\lambda = 20$ they differ by $\approx 0.04$, several times our tightest CI — but the current sweep tops out at $\lambda = 7$, where the families are still nested inside each other's intervals. Deciding between "there is a safe floor" and "there is none" requires the $\lambda \in \{10, 15, 20\}$ extremes. That is the single most consequential follow-up, and it is now a two-point experiment, not a research program.

Three — the theory's naïve law is on the ropes, not dead. Proposition 2 with fixed loss exposure forces odds growth at least linear in $\lambda$ ($\theta \ge 1$; the odds-linear form L15). The surviving odds–power fit has $\theta \approx 0.69 < 1$. Within the theory that gap has one reading: trained agents reduce their own loss exposure as $\lambda$ grows — endogenous hedging. L15 is not cleanly rejected on seven points the way it was projected to be, but it sits at the high-χ² edge of the survivors while the sub-linear fit sits at the low edge. The model's stress point localized the missing mechanism even where it didn't break — and that mechanism is checkable in stored trajectories (realized per-step loss mass should fall with $\lambda$).

FALSIFICATION PROTOCOL — PRE-COMMITTED, NOW EXECUTED ONCE The analysis script fit_scaling_laws.py shipped with this post, frozen before the sweep returned. It ingests the raw run CSV, recomputes per-λ bootstrap critical points, fits all twenty laws by CI-weighted least squares, and ranks by AICc and leave-one-λ-out cross-validation. Decision rule, stated in advance: a law survives iff every per-λ critical point falls inside its pointwise 95% band, and the winner must beat the runner-up by ΔAICc ≥ 4. Outcome on the seven-point sweep: the saturating shape survives decisively; no single law clears the ΔAICc ≥ 4 bar. Per the rule, we report the finalists rather than crown one — and name the experiment that breaks the tie.
